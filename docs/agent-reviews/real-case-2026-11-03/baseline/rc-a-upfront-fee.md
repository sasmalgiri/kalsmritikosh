## Conclusion

An advance-fee ("upfront-fee") wire-fraud scheme, March–June 2016: four men promised Victim Company A a
"standby letter of credit" (SBLC) backed by Mexican gold bonds / €1 billion, took $800,000 of an agreed $1 million
"bank fee", delivered nothing and spent the money. Three of the four were convicted by a jury on Oct. 21, 2022 and
sentenced in 2023; the fourth (Adkins) has no outcome on file.

Additional schemes charged only in the superseding indictment (R2, not repeated in later releases):
- Adkins and Mignott — "Individual Victim 3", an Iowa pork farmer, $90,000 in 2013.
- Adkins and Douglas — "Victim Company B", a biotech/cancer-research company, about $250,000 in 2015.
Whether the jury convicted on these is not stated. R3 describes the conviction only in terms of the 2016 Victim Company A scheme.

Roles: the releases give no separate role for each man (no "leader", "recruiter" and so on). They say only that "the defendants" made the representations and supplied the "phony letter from a major international bank". The record gives these differences: Gillar and Douglas faced the transacting-in-proceeds count; the forfeiture amounts differ ($1M Gillar, $219k Mignott, $44,750 Douglas), which suggests Gillar received most of the proceeds (my inference); Adkins appears in all three schemes.

## Contradictions / discrepancies

1. **Douglas's transacting-in-proceeds conviction.** R3: "Gillar and Douglas were also each convicted of one count of transacting in criminal proceeds." R4 mentions only Gillar ("Gillar was also convicted of one count of transacting in criminal proceeds"). R5 lists only "wire fraud conspiracy and four counts of wire fraud" for Douglas. So R5 leaves out a count that R3 says he was convicted of.
2. **Counts charged vs convicted.** After the superseding indictment Douglas faced 4 + 2 = 6 wire counts and Mignott 4 + 1 = 5 (R2). Each was convicted of "four counts of wire fraud" (R3). Nothing explains what happened to the other counts.
3. **Number of victims and amount.** R1: "defrauding two individual victims out of approximately $800,000". R2: "defrauded four victims of over $1 million". R3–R5: "$1 million 'upfront-fee' scheme" with $800,000 actually transferred. "$1 million" is the agreed fee, not the loss. Restitution of $1.1M (R4, R5) is closer to $800k + $90k + $250k = $1.14M, which suggests the R2 side schemes were counted (my inference).
4. **Custody.** R1: "Gillar was detained." R2 (2018): "All four defendants are currently out on bail".
5. **Ages.** Mignott is 60 in both Oct 2022 (R3) and June 2023 (R4, R5). Douglas is 49 in both Oct 2022 and July 2023. Gillar goes 50 → 51. These are plausible depending on birthdays, but they cannot be checked.
6. **Names.** "Roy Gillar" in R2, "Roy Johannes Gillar" elsewhere. Douglas is "A Monmouth County … man" in R5's headline and Mignott "A Camden County, New Jersey, man" in R4. Both counties are consistent with Freehold and Voorhees, but the releases themselves do not say so.
7. **Count terminology.** R2 says "The money laundering charges each carry …", R3 says "transacting in criminal proceeds". These are the same count under two names.
8. **SBLC backing.** R1/R2 say the letter was "backed by Mexican gold bonds". R3–R5 say "backed by either €1 billion in cash or highly lucrative Mexican gold bonds".
